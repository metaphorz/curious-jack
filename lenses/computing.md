## Computer science (computing or information science) 

Computing is a natural part of lens M but also lens E since computers require software and information encoding. Computing when seen, can be viewed as information structure and process. One sees structure or sees a process. A structure can be viewed as a database and its schema, a json file, a basic data structure such as a list, tree, or graph. A common structure is a knowlege graph (semantic network), mind map, or concept map, or even a scene graph from computer graphics. A process is any behavior, procedure or activity. Common models used to encode process are flowchart (use UML activity diagrams as in PlantUML),  finite state machines, Markov models, and Petri nets. Process is thought at a top level as a sort of metaphorical flow: control flow or data flow, or as a constraint (think equation or that which might be optimized). A control flow is a set of instructions (as in a food recipe). A data flow is a set of functions connected through arcs along which the informatin flows. 

As one example, consider a view of the woods. Thinking of it from a computing lens, we start with structure. What is the structure of the entire scene, of its components (grass, bushes, trees, other objects). Processes can be the growth of trees, the path of air moving through the leaves and branches. Indirectly, what animals live in the woods? The animals can be captured by structure and process. A control flow could be the process of clearing the forest of potentially flammable material or sustainably cutting down trees. A data flow is focused on the ingredients and elements rather than a controller: seeing a process from the standpoint of tree or brush components moving through "processing machinery". 

Making toast is a good example. Explaining the process of making toast is a control flow because we are referring to a human (controller) process. All processes may have decision points or branches. If butter is in the fridge, we can butter the toast. Otherwise, there will be no buttered toast-- a decision. A data flow method of capturing process is reminiscent of making a machine that makes toast. Toast is first cut (a cutting or slicing function) and then the slices (output from this function) are placed in the toaster function and so forth--a chain of functional operations independent of the human. The end of the process is toast but how to define or model the process depends on a data vs. control perspective.

A constraint method usually involves optimization of a processed defined by one or more equations. The process of a ball's trajectory when thrown is not best captured with data or control flow. Instead, there are conservation laws that must always be satisfied.
